# orch-authz

The single access-decision implementation.

Defaults previews can resolve several permissions and profiles from one reading of their pages. Database tests retain each row's locks, holders and provenance while requiring that the statement count stays the same as for one row.

The access engine for the nodes of the hierarchy and for the resources work runs on. It decides who holds what on an org, a project, a topic and a thread, and on a provider, an agent profile, an environment, a host, a template, an MCP server or a mount, from the stored pages upward (rows and entries, the defaults of the places above and of the system, locks, owners, roles, groups, memberships, suspension, archive, and a token's scopes and subtree), answers a whole list in the statements one question takes, says everything a refused caller lacks and who can give it, and reads and changes a page or a defaults page, a resource's among them, with its audit rows and one move of the authorization epoch. For resources it says whether a person may use one and within which options (the entries of several rows combined), answers a turn's pre-flight and a binding in one call each, filters the lists a person chooses from, says who may make what and where and who owns it, what becomes of a person's things when a membership ends or a member is moved, where an environment may be created, moved and reached, who may name somebody of another root org, and what a permission is no use without. Chats are decided too: who reads, writes in, moderates and inspects the chat of a node or the direct and group chats of a root org, and which of some people can read one. Whether a reader may take a chat away as a file is part of their standing in it (`export`): the Export row of the node for a node's chat, and for a direct or a group chat being in it, so that somebody who only inspects one as an admin reads it and does not take it away. A document is described to the engine by a `Site` of its own: its page is read and changed as a resource's is, its rows follow those of the node it is beside until somebody changes one, private means cut loose from them, and what a person holds on it is asked in the statements of one question however many documents are asked about. The requests that are the same for every kind of thing are the engine's too: a thing of any kind is found by its kind and identifier and asked what a person holds on it (`things`); a change of a page, of a place's defaults or of the system's is previewed by making it inside a trial that is undone and reading what each page gave before and after, so that it says how many pages it reaches and who gains and loses what, named as far as the caller may know them, and leaves nothing behind (`previews`; the page of a place is one page and opens nothing inside it, the defaults of a place reach the pages inside that follow); a request for access is planned (what is asked must exist, not be held, not be closed by a lock or a subscription kept to its owner, and be sendable to somebody other than the person who asks, and it is for one thing, never for a place in the stead of what is in it), is sent to the nearest who can give each thing, and is decided as the approver's own change of the page, so that it gives exactly what was asked or less and only what the approver could have given (`requests`); and a thing is handed to another person, an active member of its root org, with the person who was first staying an owner unless taken out in the same step (`transfer`). Reach is the next part and plugs into `engine` the same way. They are tested on a real PostgreSQL in `tests/previews.rs`, `tests/requests.rs` and `tests/transfer.rs`. Usage is decided here too (`caps`): who adds, changes, removes and reads a usage cap by the place it is defined in, who may let a turn past one that is reached by the row where it is defined, and how deep a person may read the use of a place. It is tested on a real PostgreSQL: table-driven suites for both sides of the access matrix, property tests over random worlds (taking an entry away never grows anybody's use, and a token never has what its user lacks), and tests that the statements of a listing do not grow with the number of its nodes or resources.

A list of models in an entry's options, and the models a place allows, name models the way every list of models does: by name, by family (`gpt-6*`) and by a group of the provider's models (`@small`), matched against the groups the provider's managers put each model in (`options::allows_model`; tested in `tests/model_groups.rs`).

What an agent may reach is worked out here too (`reach`): the entries whose holder is the agents of a place on the way to the agent's thread, read through the same chains as a person's rights (cut loose, restricted, private and locked things stay out of it), the reach policy of the topic, and the standing of the agent on each conversation, chat and document as a right to read, a right to write and, for a document alone, a right to manage (`doc.manage`: to delete it, move it, bring it back and put an earlier version back, held on the document or on the place it lies beside, holding the two below it, and told of a document that was deleted as it was, so that the agent is asked whether it may bring it back), and which places' agents a document is shared with, and as a right to copy it into its workspace (`may_download_*`: what its own thread made is its to copy, anything else only where the agents are named in the Export row of its kind); it is tested against a real PostgreSQL in `tests/reach.rs` and `tests/manage_reach.rs`. A page offers sharing with agents at three levels, the third (Can manage) to whoever holds `doc.manage` there (`giving`).

Projects inside projects ask nothing new of the engine: a role, a default and a lock are found along the path, so those of a project reach the folders and topics inside it as an org's reach the departments, which `tests/folders.rs` shows for roles, defaults, locks and archive. The agent's project is the nearest one, and the reach its policy gives takes in the projects inside it. What a whole-project announcement needs is told apart in `nodes::restricted_below`: which nodes were made private below a place, and so do not follow it.

**The doors of an environment** are rows of its own page and of no thread's: Files, Terminal, Browsers and VNC, each at View and at Manage, with the shipped values the system starts with in both placements (View to Viewers; the Manage of Files and Terminal to Developers where the environment is made from a template, to nobody on a person's machine; the Manage of Browsers and VNC to nobody). Whoever holds `environment.manage` holds the Manage of all four without being named in them, which is the one thing that crosses areas. A thread's three choices name its session and nothing of its environment; the environment's Can view is its own View row and the View row of each door. Who reaches into an environment is told by the door that lets them in, at the highest level they hold, or by an agent there that they may talk to (`environments::reach`); how many people read the files of a thread's environment, whatever its page says, is `environments::reaches`; and what sharing a thread offers beside it, looking in and for whoever sets the thread up the Manage of Files and Terminal, never the environment and never the Manage of Browsers or VNC, is `doors`, given where the caller holds it and asked for where they do not. A shell is asked of `terminal.manage` on the environment, whichever thread it was opened beside. They are tested on a real PostgreSQL in `tests/doors.rs` and `tests/environments.rs`.

Who may give what, and how much, is one rule (`giving`). Naming somebody further in a row one holds, one of the three everyday choices, a role in a place, a row of a defaults page, a share with agents and the decision of a request for access each ask it, and a page tells the same thing beforehand (`can_give` on a row, `gives` on a page, `can_give` on the roles of a place), so that a form offers nothing that would be refused. Sharing takes holding what is given and, for somebody who neither owns the thing nor manages its page, that the place lets holders share (the policy `sharing.allow_holders`); managing the page (taking out what others gave, cutting a row loose, Private, a lock, every change of a defaults page) takes `access.manage`; a sharer takes back what they gave and not what another gave, for as long as they could give it again; and a change that gives more than its giver holds is refused whole, with everything they lack. The three Export rows ride with the three everyday choices as far as the giver holds them: a row the giver cannot give is left out of the change, never a reason to refuse it. What the page of a place covers is counted in the same few statements however many threads lie inside it (`covers`: the places, threads and documents inside that follow the place, and those made private or cut loose, as far as the caller may see them). They are tested on a real PostgreSQL in `tests/giving.rs`, `tests/covers.rs`, `tests/reaches.rs` (a role in a place, a name on its own page and a name on one document each reach what they should, for a person, a group and somebody of another root org) and `tests/manage.rs` (Manage is the thing itself, of every kind of thing).

| [environments](src/environments.rs) | An environment's page of two levels and four doors, whose Manage row takes entries on a person's machine as anywhere; managing as deciding where threads run in it; the defaults its page takes by where it runs; everybody who can reach into it, by its doors and by an agent there that they may talk to; and a shell as its opener's alone, closed by whoever manages the environment. |
| [resources](src/resources.rs) | A resource's page and visibility, its home at any level of orgs and who may make one there, what is usable by whoever acts (the author of an input, whoever puts a thread somewhere, the thread's first owner for an input no person sent), hosts, and who may add a mount's location. |

| [external](src/external.rs) | Naming somebody from outside the root org on the page of a node, a resource or a document: the row that takes, or owning the thing where the policy lets owners; who names the holders of that row; from then on it is access like anybody's, wherever they work. |
| [missing](src/missing.rs) | Everything a caller lacks for an action, the nearest people who can give each part and how many more could, whether the caller may force it as an admin of the place, and what nobody can give and why: for refusals, the watching card and access requests. |
| [things](src/things.rs) | Any thing that has a page, found by its kind and identifier: where its page stands, and what a person holds on it, the same for a node, a resource and a document. |
| [requests](src/requests.rs) | Access requests: what a person asks for on a thing they lack, the nearest people it is sent to, what a decision gives, and when what was asked for is held. |
| [giving](src/giving.rs) | Who may give what, and how much: the one rule behind an entry in a row, one of the three choices, a role in a place, a row of a defaults page, a share with agents and the decision of a request for access, which a page also tells beforehand. |
| [covers](src/covers.rs) | What a role held in a place, or a share of the place with what is in it, reaches: the places, threads and documents inside that still follow the place, and those made private or cut loose throughout, as far as the caller may see them. |
| [previews](src/previews.rs) | The effect a change of a page would have, worked out before it is made: how many pages it reaches and who gains and who loses what on them, counted and named as far as the caller may know them. |

| [depends](src/depends.rs) | What a permission is no use without on another object, whether the people concerned have it, and giving it or asking for it in the same step. |
| [chats](src/chats.rs) | Chat access by a node's page, by membership or guest status within a root org, and by inspection for whoever holds that row on an org. |
| [documents](src/documents.rs) | A document's page: the Documents rows of its node followed until changed (view, create, comment, edit, manage), private until shared, who makes and moves one, and what an agent may read and edit, where Edit on a place is what makes documents there. |
| [doors](src/doors.rs) | What sharing a thread offers beside it of its environment: looking into it, and for whoever sets the thread up the Manage of its files and its shells; whether the people named have that, giving it in the same step where the giver may, and asking for it where they may not. |
| [reach](src/reach.rs) | What an agent may read, write and send, kind by kind: the reach policy, and entries by area whose holder is the agents of a thread or of a place, inherited on both sides; a chat and a thread each written to by a right of its own. |
| [caps](src/caps.rs) | Who may add, change and remove a usage cap; who may let a turn past one that is reached, by the row where that cap is defined; and who sees how much was used: one's own in full, a place's totals by its row, a place's reports. |
